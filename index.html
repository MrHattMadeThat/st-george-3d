About this stage

A cartoon model of the land around St. George as it was in the Granite Town years (1874–76), made as a stage for short animations about the granite trade: quarry, canal, river, sheds, wharf and ship.

How it was made

  • Shorelines, lakes, rivers and marshes come from OpenStreetMap. Hills come from the AWS Terrain Tiles elevation model. Both are modern and have been simplified.
  • Hills are drawn taller than life (the “Hill height” slider) so the land reads at a glance.
  • Farms and clearings are drawn only around settlements of the 1870s; everything else is forest, as it mostly was.
  • The red granite belt and its ledges follow Gwen Martin’s Maps 2 and 3.
  • There are no buildings, roads or railways yet. (The Grand Southern “Shore Line” railway was only completed through St. George in 1880.)

Known differences from 1874

  • Above the falls, the river is drawn at today’s level. The pulp mill dam of 1902 flooded the ravine called The Gulley; in 1874 it was dry ground.
  • The falls are simplified into one cascade. Martin’s Map 4 shows upper falls, lower falls and the Gorge.
  • Names marked * are placed by eye from maps or written descriptions.
